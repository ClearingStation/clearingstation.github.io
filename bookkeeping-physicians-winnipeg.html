Sound Familiar?

Most doctors who look for physician bookkeeping in Winnipeg say one of these.

"My remittance never matches what I billed."

Fee-for-service payments arrive from Manitoba Health, WCB and third parties. Nobody ties them back to your claims.

"I don't know if I take salary or dividends."

You picked one when you incorporated. You are not sure it still fits your income or your plans.

"Money keeps building up in my corporation."

Your MPC holds more each year. You don't know if it is invested and reported the right way.

"My spouse does the books at night."

Overhead payments, receipts, CRA instalments. It all lands on the kitchen table after a long day.

"My bookkeeper doesn't get physician finances."

They ask what an overhead split is. They treat your practice like a retail shop.

"I file two returns and understand neither."

A T2 for the corporation, a personal return for you. You sign both and hope they line up.

Should I incorporate as a physician in Manitoba?

It depends on your income, your spending and your stage, for example a resident finishing training or a locum. There is no single answer. On the call, Jennifer tells you what to look at for your situation.

Should I pay myself salary or dividends from my MPC?

Each has trade-offs. Salary creates RRSP room and CPP contributions; dividends do not. The right mix depends on your income and plans, so we look at your numbers before we answer.

Do physicians charge GST?

Most medical services are exempt from GST. Some services, such as certain medical-legal reports or cosmetic work, can be taxable. We check what applies to your billings.
