## What catch-up bookkeeping includes

Catch-up bookkeeping brings your books from "behind" to "current" in one project. For each month that is behind, a bookkeeper:

1. Gets your bank and credit card statements for the month.
2. Records and categorizes every transaction.
3. Reconciles each account, so the books match the bank to the cent.
4. Finds missing documents and asks you only for the ones that matter.
5. Prepares the GST/PST and payroll filings that are overdue.

When the books are current, any overdue tax returns can be filed on numbers you can defend.

## What drives the price

Four things decide most of the cost:

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Months behind | Each month needs its own reconciliation and close. |
| Transactions per month | 40 transactions a month is a very different job from 400. |
| Number of accounts | Every bank account, credit card and loan is reconciled separately. |
| Payroll and GST/PST | Overdue remittances and returns add work, and they are often the most urgent part. |

What does **not** change the price much: how messy the shoebox is. Most of the work comes from bank data, not from paper.

## How CRA penalties add up while you wait

For income tax, the CRA charges a late-filing penalty of **5% of the balance owing, plus 1% for each full month the return is late, up to 12 months**. If the CRA sent you a demand to file and you also had a late-filing penalty in one of the three previous years, it can rise to 10% plus 2% per month, up to 20 months. Interest is charged on top, compounded daily.

GST/HST returns have their own late-filing penalty, based on the amount owing and the number of months late.

Source: [CRA – Penalties and interest](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/interest-penalties.html)

If you are entitled to a refund, there is usually no late-filing penalty. In that case, the CRA owes you money and catching up gets it back.
